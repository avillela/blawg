I’ve done a LOT of business travel this year — 10 trips in 2025! As I write the initial draft of post, I’m sitting on a plane from Tokyo to Toronto, returning from my last trip of the year — [Open Source Summit Japan](https://events.linuxfoundation.org/open-source-summit-japan/program/schedule/). I’d say that I’ve become a pretty seasoned traveler over the last year.

One of the perks of conference travel is getting to make new friends from all over the world. Of course, it means that I usually only get to see them if we attend the same conferences. I’ve been lucky enough to hang out a bunch with [Josh Lee](https://www.linkedin.com/in/joshuamlee/) this fall. He was my co-speaker in 4 different cities, including this last trip to Tokyo. A couple of trips ago, we started exchanging notes on what we pack for business trips, and got to talking about our must-have travel items. So we came up with the idea of doing some cross-linking blog posts about our packing must-haves.

Here are mine!

### What’s in Adriana’s bag?

#### Luggage

I always travel with a carry-on and a backpack, no matter how long the trip. I’ve been on a couple of 2-week trips this year, and still…carryon. Reasons why I prefer to travel with a carry-on:

*   Way easier to deal with if you need to be rebooked on a new flight due to a missed connection
*   I don’t trust the airlines to not lose my luggage
*   Easier exit from the airport because you don’t need to wait for your bags at the baggage carousel

I stick with packing essentials. Enough undies, socks, and undershirts (support tank tops) for the trip, 1 pair of jeans (plus the pair I wear to travel), and a rotation of 3–4 non-bulky sweaters/long sleeve tops. Oh, and of course, pajamas.

My travel shoes are usually waterproof, in case I travel to a rainy place, and multi-purpose (i.e. make me look good when I’m at a conference). I try to save room in my luggage by packing at most one other pair of shoes, besides slippers for the hotel. If I do pack an extra pair of shoes in my suitcase, I try to choose non-bulky ones that are comfortable and look stylish. I have standards! Having small feet also helps. 🙃

I use [packing cubes](https://www.mec.ca/en/product/6029-377/mec-travel-light-packing-cube-3-pack-multisize?colour=Neptune%2FSalmonberry%2FAlpin&gad_source=1&gad_campaignid=23148870646&gbraid=0AAAAAD-dszhGUHgw8blCmF-9TsA-aO7NJ&gclid=Cj0KCQiAxonKBhC1ARIsAIHq_lteZ2UH4-uzdwrd_Q1FxvVUKW5Iy9CjakrRpUgT7podBwrENKFcnZMaAs5NEALw_wcB) to help organize my clothes. I have a love/hate relationship with vacuum bags. They’re great for packing more clothes/bulkier clothes, but they don’t pack well inside the suitcase. I also pack my sunglasses (and hard case — made the mistake of not packing the hard case once and broke my sunglasses in transit), and a daypack. I’m a HUGE fan of packable daypacks. If I’m at a conference and I’m not speaking, I prefer to just bring a daypack with me, instead of my bulky-ass backpack. It comes in handy for stashing snacks, a water bottle, and even my jacket, if I get too warm.

I had a great packable daypack that I picked up in Bergen, Norway this fall, but it was attached to my backpack on my last trip on my way home, and it fell off along the way. I’m in mourning. 😭

#### Toiletries

Fitting liquids into those teeny little airport zipper bags is a pain in the ass and always causes me a lot of stress, so I try to pack bare essentials:

*   Contact lens solution (not the 60ml, but the 100ml, which usually lasts 2 weeks)
*   Mouth wash
*   Toothpaste
*   Moisturizer
*   Leave-in conditioner (makes up for those shitty hotel shampoo and conditioner sets that dry out your hair)

Also, fun fact: liquid medicine doesn’t have to go in those stupid tiny bags, so that can save you some room.

As a contact lens wearer, I also always pack 2 pairs of spare contacts. I was once on a trip to Brazil with my sister when we were teenagers and one of her contacts ripped. She didn’t have a spare, so she was stuck with glasses for the rest of the trip. Lesson learned.

As a nighttime retainer wearer, I also pack a bunch of retainer cleaning tablets. I save on space by using a 1/4 tab per day. Seems to get the job done.

I don’t wear any makeup (lip balm doesn’t count), so that obviously saves me a lot of room too. My daughter loves wearing makeup, and I’ve noticed that when she travels, she packs travel versions of: concealer, mascara, eye shadow palette, makeup brushes, primer, blush, and lip gloss.

Additional stuff:

*   Bandaids
*   Alcohol wipes
*   Advil
*   Gravol (the [chewey ginger gummies](https://gravol.ca/en/product/ginger-lozenges/))
*   Mini hair brush
*   Tooth brush
*   Floss
*   Razor (yes, they’re allowed)
*   Contact lens case

It’s come in handy a few times in hotels that don’t have a nightstand power outlet. They try to be clever by having USB-A ports, which are useless to me, because all of my devices are USB-C. When hotel rooms _do_ have USB-C, it’s never enough to power my charging pad. So in that case, I plug my charging pad to my Anker power bank overnight, and then charge the power bank in the morning. Best of all, the power bank charges FAST, so it’s ready for the next evening.

It also comes handy when you’re at a conference, are low on power on your laptop, and can’t find an outlet. Best. Purchase. Ever.

### Outerwear

I always stress about what jacket to wear, especially if I’m going from a cold place to a warmer place, and vice-versa. I’ve taken to packing layers — a rain shell, a fleece, and a puffer, when I travel in winter/fall/early spring. Layering is a must when traveling from warmer places to cooler places and vice-versa. It’s a bit awkward to carry around so many jackets when traveling, but at least I can mix and match, depending on the weather situation.

### Purse

As a girl, I can get away with taking a small purse on the plane. It comes in handy for stashing my phone, passport, wallet, and AirPods. I also keep a foldable tote bag and extra daypack in there.

When I’m out and about, I also throw my wireless magnetic phone charger and watch charger in there.

### Final thoughts

Traveling can be stressful and annoying. I get MASSIVE anxiety every time I travel, and the best way I’ve found to manage it is by having a plan and a packing list.

Whether you’re just starting out on business travel, or have been doing it for a while, hope you picked up something useful today. I’d love to hear what’s in YOUR bag!

Cheers to a smooth travel year for 2026!
