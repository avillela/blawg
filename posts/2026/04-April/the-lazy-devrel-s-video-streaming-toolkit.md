I’ve been in Developer Relations (DevRel) for about 4 years now, and you may be surprised to know that for most of this time, I’ve had a very basic audio and video setup. I’m neither an audiophile nor a videophile, so the fancy-dancy setup has never really appealed to me, and always seemed a bit pretentious.

Case in point, I’ve spent most of my 4 years as a DevRel happily using my MacBook Pro’s built-in webcam for streams, online talks, and podcasts. The most I ever did was get [a podcasting mic](https://rode.com/en-ca/products/nt-usb-plus) when I started, because [I got thrown into podcasting almost right away](https://open.spotify.com/show/4ZI6pQwChwm4sVULdtHFMe), and [our producer](https://www.linkedin.com/in/mandylee1116/) suggested I use something better than my Mac’s built-in mic or my [AirPods Pro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AirPods_Pro) for audio.

### Time for a change

Fast forward to 2026. This is the year that I decided to change things up a bit. I’ve been producing more video content in the last year, so I decided it was time to upgrade to a proper external webcam.

As I said before, I typically use my laptop webcam for video streams. While it’s “good enough”, the webcam doesn’t sit at eye level for me, which means that I don’t tend to make eye contact with the camera when I record video. It would be nice to set up a camera behind my computer, at eye level, for a more polished video look. My laptop’s camera also doesn’t record 4k video, which these days seems to be a must. 🤷‍♀

I have some really really nice DSLR cameras and some REALLY nice lenses kicking around from my professional photographer days: [Canon 5D Mark III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canon_EOS_5D_Mark_III) (full-frame) and [Canon 7D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canon_EOS_7D) (cropped frame). I also have an [iPhone 17 Pro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IPhone_17_Pro), which also has a pretty great camera. Ideally, I would’ve liked to have connected one of these cameras to my work laptop. Technically, I could, but here’s the rub:

*   **To use my iPhone camera, I needed to use the same AppleID on my work laptop and on my personal phone.** No, with a side order of nope. I like to keep my work assets and my personal assets separate.
*   **To use any of my Canon cameras, I would’ve had to install a special Canon driver on my work laptop.** While I was able to install the driver, I was unable to enable it, because that required a level of admin access on my work machine that I did not have, nor would I ever be allowed to get.

Back to the drawing board.

### New toys!!!

After chatting with a few DevRel friends, here is the equipment that I ended up with:

[**RØDE NT USB microphone**](https://www.amazon.ca/dp/B00KQPGRRE) **(CAD$338.28).** As I said earlier, the microphone is one of the early pieces of DevRel equipment that I invested in. Definitely worth it. It came with a [USB-B to USB-A cable](https://www.amazon.ca/USB-Cable-Male-20Ft-Superspeed/dp/B07PWK5K92?th=1), and I am a USB-C girlie. (Seriously, why are we still using USB-B for everything??). So I bought a separate [USB-B to UCB-C cable](https://www.amazon.ca/dp/B07DCNXWND) (CAD$10.99) instead, so I could plug it directly into my laptop without a USB-B to USB-C adapter.

[**Boom arm for microphone**](https://www.amazon.ca/dp/B0BCJ55R2L) **(CAD$69.99).** The RØDE mic comes with a little desktop stand, which is fine, but it was a bit awkward and I got tired of it, so I decided to get myself a boom arm for my mic. I did some research to make sure that it was compatible with my mic, since it wasn’t a Røde-specific boom arm.

![A close-up of a RØDE microphone on a boom arm attached to a wooden desk, with a monitor, laptop, and office equipment blurred in the background.](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/1*zFpz1_sXXjsXgdcA3rtj_A.jpeg)

My RØDE NT USB mic on the boom arm

[**Sony ZV1F webcam**](https://www.amazon.ca/dp/B0BHKKHNHM?th=1) **(CAD$548).** This was recommended to me by [Justin Garrison](https://bsky.app/profile/justingarrison.com), who has made his fair share of video content. Check out his podcast, [Fork Around and Find Out (FAFO)](https://fafo.fm), with my awesome friend, [Autumn Nash](https://youtu.be/ahfcP66_cEk). ([I was a guest on FAFO last year](https://youtu.be/HvhU7AIhvgs?si=q0Oe-w3EMbt7Hv6B).) The camera is great because it plugs in via USB, and doesn’t require a special driver. There were some additional surprises around the camera setup, however. I’ll talk about those shortly.

_PS: You do NOT need an SD card if you’re streaming video._

[**Elgato teleprompter**](https://www.amazon.ca/dp/B0CH3P9K1X) **(CAD$389.99).** Having the webcam at eye level is great, but when you’re recording something scripted, it’s very helpful to have a teleprompter so that you look at the camera as you speak. The nice thing about this teleprompter is that you can mount a camera behind it. It actually comes with a little plate that you can attach to the back of the prompter, on which your camera can sit. The plate comes with a screw so that you can attach the bottom of your camera onto it. It also has an opening at the back of the teleprompter for the camera lens.

My friend and teammate, [Henrik Rexed](https://www.youtube.com/c/isitobservable), recommended this teleprompter, and from talking to several DevRel friends, this seems to be \*the\* teleprompter that everyone has.

![A compact teleprompter setup on a desk, showing a Sony camera behind reflective glass with a mic and ring light nearby.](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/1*FxlrdSziNd41TASrnTvdgg.jpeg)

My Sony ZV1F webcam mounted on my Elgato telemprompter

The Elgato comes with software which runs in either text mode (teleprompter) or in screen mode. Screen mode allows you to use the Elgato as a (very small) second monitor for your streaming software (e.g. Zoom, Streamyard) so that you can maintain eye contact while you stream/record.

![A teleprompter on a desk reflecting a computer screen, framed by a ring light. Small desk toys surround it, including a yellow rubber chicken, a tiny bird figure, a waving lucky cat, and a Lego-style figure on a box labeled “Telemetry,” with a “HOW TO FLY” sign in the background.](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/1*IrzSufHdrG543KrtYD3GOw.jpeg)

The Elgato teleprompter serves as a second monitor so you can keep eye contact while streaming

[**Desk ring light**](https://www.amazon.ca/dp/B088LLK89G) **(CAD$49.99).** I only have a single overhead light plus the natural light coming in from my window in my home office. This is fine during the day, but not so great when it gets dark or on gloomy days. Having the desk ring light is super handy for adding extra lighting. I also find that with my camera mounted behind my teleprompter, the reflection from the teleprompter’s screen causes it to cast a weird light back on the camera lens, affecting the colour on the video, so the ring light helps correct that.

Some folks have fancy light setups with light boxes. Good for them. This is good enough for me, and it does the trick.

### Surprise purchases

When I bought my teleprompter and webcam, I thought that was all that I needed. I should’ve known better. When it comes to A/V equipment, there’s always extra stuff involved.

Here are some of the additional things that I required:

[**A tripod for my teleprompter**](https://www.amazon.ca/dp/B0DCVX8QRT?ref=ppx_pop_mob_ap_share&th=1) **(CAD$69.99).** The Elgato doesn’t come with one, so I had to get a small desktop tripod to mount it on.

[**A dummy battery for my webcam**](https://www.amazon.ca/dp/B0C6YDC52T?ref=ppx_pop_mob_ap_share) **(CAD$23.41).** The Sony ZV1F is powered by battery. This is fine if you use it as a regular camera. But I intend to use it as a stationary camera for streaming, so it needs to be on for long stretches. This means that I don’t want my battery to either die or overheat in the middle of a stream or recording. The solution, according to the interwebs/AI overlords is to use a [dummy battery](https://filmgearaccessories.co.za/blogs/news/everything-you-need-to-know-about-dummy-batteries-power-your-camera-without-limits?srsltid=AfmBOoqQ_tg67KvFFWYkQ7g3t3pDSH5qm2vBOHBq9ypJ87HG41jMXUBh). A dummy battery looks like a battery with a cord coming out of it. You put the battery into the camera’s battery slot, and you plug the cord into a USB power adapter. It never charges. It’s just direct A/C power.

The dummy battery works really well, but there’s one dumb thing about the design of the Sony webcam. You can’t really close the battery port when you use the dummy battery. There’s no passthrough for the cord. (As far as I can tell — I’ve looked!) Fortunately, the fact that it doesn’t close doesn’t keep the camera from operating properly. My workaround was to use duct tape to hold the battery port door closed-ish.

![A close-up of a camera with HDMI and other cables plugged in, with a colorful bit of tape near the bottom and a blue “WASHINGTON ADRIANA” license plate blurred in the background.](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/1*WWY6PCB011JVPmsmCxuiVQ.jpeg)

My duct tape workaround for the stupid cable situation with the dummy battery

[**Micro HDMI cable**](https://www.amazon.ca/dp/B0G1SFHHHN) **(CAD$20.51).** The Sony ZV1F comes with a USB-C cable, which you can connect to your computer to stream video. That’s all well and good, but the best video output you’ll get is 720p. If you want 4k output, you need an HDMI cable. But not just any HDMI cable. For the Sony, you need a _micro HDMI cable_. (Fun fact, HDMI seems to come in 3 sizes: micro \[Type D\], mini \[Type C\], and standard \[Type A\]. WHY, OH WHY??)

You can either buy a regular HDMI cable _and_ get a micro HDMI (male) to HDMI adapter (female), or just get a micro HDMI to HDMI cable (both ends male). [I ended up getting the second one](https://www.amazon.ca/dp/B0G1SFHHHN), as it involved fewer purchases.

And just when I thought I was done, I also needed…

[**4K USB-C video capture card**](https://www.amazon.ca/dp/B0FM4JK2PT?ref=ppx_pop_mob_ap_share&th=1) **(CAD$38.88).** When I got my HDMI cable, I thought I could just plug it into my laptop’s HDMI port directly from my webcam. 🚫 Wrong! 🚫 Nope. Turns out that the HDMI port on a laptop is video out, and I needed video in. And that’s where the video capture card comes into play. I got a 4K version because I wanted to stream in 4K video. The video capture card is HDMI (female) to USB-C (male), and connects to the camera’s HDMI cable.

Now, I got a pretty cheap video capture card. You can get some really fancy ones. One that many folks recommend is [this Elgato video capture card](https://www.amazon.ca/Elgato-4K-External-Passthrough-Near-Zero/dp/B0FFTFYGLV/) (CAD$219.99). Maybe someday I’ll get a nicer one, but for now, my little cheapie one did the trick.

My camera’s micro-HDMI to HDMI cable connected to the USB-C video capture card

#### And the cherry on top…

Oh, but we’re not done quite yet. Because you see, that’s a lot of USB cables — 5, in fact — to connect to my MacBook pro. Six, if you include also my power cord. But my MacBook Pro only has _three_ USB-C ports. So what’s a gal to do? Get more gadgets, that’s what!

I ended up getting a little [**powered USB-C hub to plug in all of my devices**](https://www.amazon.ca/dp/B0G13RQ2SZ?ref=ppx_pop_mob_ap_share) **(CAD$119)**. There are TONS of options for USB-C powered hubs. Believe me. I scoured Amazon for a few hours to find the “perfect” one for me.

If you want to get extra fancy, you could opt for a docking station with support for multiple HDMI ports for multiple external monitors. I only have one external monitor, and I use it every few weeks, so hard pass for me.

I wanted something relatively small and compact, and that had a bunch of USB-C ports. Most of the ones I checked out had a bunch of USB-B ports (WHYYYYYY???), one peripheral USB-C port and 2 USB-C power ports. ARRRRGH!! Kind of infurating. Anyway, the one I ended up buying has a nice mix of USB-B and USB-C ports, and has individual power buttons for each port.

![A USB hub on a desk with several labeled cables plugged in — tags reading charger, mic, camera, camera charger, and ring light — next to a tripod mount with foam grips and orange knobs, with a sticker‑covered wooden box in the background.](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/1*Vcth3-4Fp1OW8BXcioauFQ.jpeg)

My lovely USB-C and USB-B powered hub

I also took my friend [Josh Lee’](https://www.joshuamlee.com/about/)s advice and got some [labels for my various cables](https://www.amazon.ca/dp/B0DM5LG3LP) (CAD$13.49), so I’d know what was what. I’m sure Josh would have a heart attack looking at the mess of cables, but this is relatively tidy, all things considered.

### Final Thoughts

I really didn’t know what I was getting into when I embarked on this journey of getting a dedicated webcam for work. There were definitely some surprises and learnings, and it took way longer to get things set up the way I wanted, but now that it’s all set up, I’m pretty happy with it. Here’s the final setup!
