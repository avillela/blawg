### The perpetual hamster wheel

When I started in DevRel in 2022, I had already been in tech for 21 years. Tech was not new to me, but DevRel was. Nobody knew who I was, so I had to make a name for myself. I put myself out there. I hustled hard. I blogged about cool things that I learned. I posted regularly on socials. I submitted talks to conferences. I [started a podcast](https://bio.site/geekingout). It worked. I got noticed!

As my work gained more visibility, people started to know who I was. That translated to getting invited to be on other people’s podcasts. Getting invited to be a talking head on some tech panels. Speaking at more and more conferences. And the cherry on top? Being invited to keynote at a conference in Portugal, my ancestral home. My employer was happy with my work. My ego was inflated. I was on top of the world. I should’ve been happy. But I wasn’t. What was wrong with me??

Making things worse was the start of each calendar year, otherwise known as the day that the imaginary counter in my head hit reset and I had to prove myself all over again. My heart filled with dread. Could I top Last Year Me’s accomplishments? Would I publish as many blog posts? Would I get accepted to speak in as many conferences? What if I didn’t get accepted to speak at KubeCon again? (HOW EMBARRASSING) What if I ran out of things to say? The conversations that we have in our heads are just wild, aren’t they? And yet, I believed all that negative self-talk.

Never mind that I did super well the previous calendar year. And the year before that. And the year before that. Never mind that I got [Most Active Speaker on Sessionize for 3 years in a row](https://sessionize.com/adrianamvillela). Nope. It didn’t matter. The elation I felt for each thing I accomplished was only temporary. As soon as I accomplished one thing, I looked for The Next Great Thing to top The Previous Great Thing that I’d accomplished. There was no time to savour those victories. They were left in the past. Barely an echo. I needed to have Another Great Year to prove myself worthy of having a seat at the table. But to whom? The imaginary audience who keeps track of every single fucking thing that I do? Who exactly was I trying to impress??

You probably won’t be surprised to hear that at this pace, burnout hit me every few months. I mean, it’s unsustainable. Each burnout was accompanied by some, fuck-it-I’m-dead-and-I-need-to-reset time. And after each post-burnout recovery, I vowed to never let the burnout take hold again. And then I got back on the hamster wheel and did it all over again.

### **Breaking point**

This year, the start-of-calendar-year funk hit extra hard, promptly followed by burnout. I found myself in distress. I questioned my career. What was I doing? What was the point of all this? What was I running toward? Why did I keep trying to please the imaginary audience in my head?

I got sick and took a couple of sick days from work. A rarity for me, since I still mask indoors, so you can imagine what it took to get sick. In March, I went on two back-to-back European trips for work, with 3.5 days at home between trips. My last trip was to [Amsterdam for KubeCon](https://medium.com/dzerolabs/kubecon-eu-2026-retrospective-c6908229172e), and while I had a wonderful time, there were times during that trip when I felt lost, in spite of being surrounded by friends. That was the tipping point for me. I had a serious chat with my good friend Carmen Huidobro about this. I wanted to talk to someone who knew what I was going through.

We talked about the constant pressures of DevRel. Being present on socials and being penalized by “the algorithm” when you’re not. I told her how I felt upset when my LinkedIn posts didn’t get “enough likes and impressions”. How someone’s post mentioning me got more posts than my own posts. How elated I felt when my social posts got lots of love. And how I recognized how stupid it was that I was feeling all this. How stupid it was to have my feelings dictated by engagement.

Carmen has gone through a DevRel existential crisis herself, and has made some changes to her life as a result. She ended up deciding to step back from DevRel, taking on a different role. You can read about her journey [here](https://carmenh.dev/2026/04/08/becoming-a-chief-technology-officer/).

But me? I love DevRel and I don’t see myself doing anything else. Of all the roles I’ve had over the last couple of decades — tester, software developer, team lead, manager, and DevRel — DevRel has been one true love. But it’s also been killing me. How could I keep going on like this?

### Making changes

As I said, I hit a breaking point. For me, it wasn’t just the realization that I couldn’t keep doing this over and over again. It was the realization that I’m not getting any younger. My only child is going away to university in the fall. (Where did the last 18 years go??) I’m closer to the end of my career than I am to the beginning of it. Retirement is still a bit off for me, but it doesn’t mean that my life should revolve around work until then. I don’t want to be one of those people whose best years passed them by because they were buried in work. I love my work, but I don’t want to be defined by it.

So I’ve started making some changes. It’s a work in progress. There are ups and downs, but I started, and I’ll keep trying. And who knows…you might find them useful too!

#### 1- Go to therapy

I’ve been going to therapy for a while now. I started going to help me cope with the death of my mom in 2022. I continue to go as a type of “mental maintenance”. I don’t see my therapist weekly like I used to, but I still see her once a month. I’m glad that I do. She helps me put things into perspective. She gives me the tools that I need to cope with stress. And she does her appointments virtually, so I don’t waste time commuting to and from her office, making it a lot easier for me to stick with therapy.

#### 2- Take breaks

I am _terrible_ at taking breaks. My ADHD keeps me hyper-fixated when I’m in the middle of something that I enjoy (e.g. writing this blog post). It also causes me to “guilt work” if I feel like I haven’t been productive enough, causing me to not take breaks even when my butt is screaming at me to get off my chair (like it is right now).

I’m trying to be better about taking breaks and being okay with leaving something half-done before going on a break. It’s still hard. It’s a work in progress, but I’ll keep at it.

#### 3- Say “no” more often

This one is hard. But then my very wise husband said something that really stuck with me:

> Saying “no” to something means being able to say “yes” to something down the road.

We don’t need to say yes to EVERY. SINGLE. REQUEST. I know… THE HORROR! I live with the perpetual fear and anxiety that if I decline an opportunity, I will be forgotten. Out of sight, out of mind! But do you know what’s worse than that? Missing out on time with your loved ones because you’re always hustling for your career.

As a quick example. I was asked to keynote at a conference in Kuala Lumpur, Malaysia, in June. I was ready to say “yes”, until I realized that it coincided with my daughter’s high school graduation. It was an easy “no” for me at that point. There’s no way in hell that I’d be missing out on this important milestone in her life.

#### 4- Keep a list of your accomplishments

I started keeping a list of my accomplishments a few years ago. I have a Google Spreadsheet with separate tabs for each year. Every blog post I write, every podcast episode I publish, every talk I give…you get the point.

Doing so helps you in two ways:

1.  It comes in handy for your end-of-year self-evaluation!
2.  You can take a look at your accomplishments at the end of the year and revel in them.

Which leads me to my next point…

#### 5- Savour your accomplishments

Okay, this is sooooo hard for me! As I said before, once I accomplish one thing, I’m already thinking about the next. Take the time to take a victory lap and celebrate The Awesome Thing that you’ve done. You deserve it. I need to remind myself of that one too.

#### 6- Stop comparing yourself to others

Oooh, another tough one for me. My team at work is full of super high-achieving folks. They produce awesome content and they hustle hard. Sometimes I look at the work that they do and think that I don’t fit in. They’re so much more knowledgeable in X, Y, and Z compared to me.

So I’m trying to reframe things a bit. It starts with admitting that, no, I don’t fit in. Because I don’t fit into a mould. **I am an individual and I bring my own skills and talents to the team**. That’s why I’m there and that’s why I’m valued. Make sure that you remember that too!

#### 7- Find a hobby you love that has nothing to do with work

I love bouldering. I try to get to the bouldering gym 2–3 times per week. It is my happy place. I can hang out solo, or join a group of random strangers and work on the same problem together. Plus, bouldering is great for my ADHD, because it keeps my mind from thinking about work stuff.

Maybe bouldering isn’t for you. Not a problem. Love to draw? Want to take up a new instrument or dust off an old one? Love to garden? Lean into it!

#### 8- Turn off from work

The end of the workday means the start of your personal time. Don’t check email, Slack, Teams, etc. This sets boundaries for you and for your coworkers, who know not to bother you after hours. This also means not thinking about work after work, because that also counts as work.

Easier said than done, I know. I’m still struggling with that one. Yesterday, after I finished my workday, little work things kept creeping into my mind. One thing that’s helped me is to create a to-do list for the next day at the end of your workday. Or dump thoughts into a paper note (the horror!) so that you purge them from your brain.

And stay away from your phone as much as possible after hours. I’ve been getting into the habit of keeping my phone as far away from me as possible in the evenings, so that I’m not tempted to mindlessly check it if I get bored. If I do get bored, I read my Kindle (which I keep nearby), put the TV on (don’t judge — I like my TV), or start a conversation with my family (GASP).

#### 9- It’s okay to drop some balls

Sometimes we have too much stuff on our plates, and something’s gotta give. I’ve had to pull back on a few things recently, in order to achieve more balance. For example, when I first released my podcast, I released it weekly. Then last year, I reduced it to 2 episodes per month. While that helped, it was still too much work, especially since I’m mostly a one-woman show. So I reduced my frequency to one episode per month. While I’m sad to have had to pull back, I don’t regret my decision at all, as it has given me some much-needed breathing room.

#### 10- Be kind to yourself

You’re gonna have good days and bad days. Sometimes you’ll be winning at life, and other times you’ll be sucking at All the Things. Be kind to yourself. Know that it happens, and when it does, take a breather, and try again.

Being kind to yourself also means drowning out the negative self-talk. I read somewhere recently that it’s helpful to give your negative thoughts a name. As in personify them. That way, you can tell <Person> to fuck off. It’s so freeing!

### You’re not alone

When I told Carmen that I was planning on writing a blog post about how I’ve been feeling lately, she asked me if I was doing it for the likes. Fair question! I’ll be honest – the likes feel nice! But I’ve been thinking about this for a while now and I wanted to put it into words.

Blogging is very therapeutic for me. When [I posted about losing my mom](https://medium.com/@adri-v/i-will-remember-you-d287a8dba075), it was not only helpful to me, it was also helpful to others who had complex relationships with loved ones whom they’d lost.

When I wrote, [From “I Don’t Know” to “Let Me Show You How”](https://medium.com/womenintechnology/from-i-dont-know-to-let-me-show-you-how-4be0c79e57c8), I was pleasantly surprised by how much it resonated with folks. To me, it shows that people are feeling the same types of feelings that I am, and they’re comforted to know that they’re not alone.

I’m still figuring things out. I am still filled with anxiety and dread and fear of not being good enough or of fading out of the spotlight. But I keep trying every single day to fight the negative voices in my head and to put systems in place to help me avoid the next burnout, because time is precious and I don’t want to squander it.

I hope this helps you too.
