It's evening. I'm sitting by the fireplace, enjoying a cup of green tea. I'm finally home after having been away for almost 2 weeks. On this last trip, I travelled to 3 different countries, spoke at 4 different conferences, and gave 5 different talks, 3 of which were keynotes. I'm exhausted and jet lagged, but my heart is full. I got to hang out with some of my good friends in the Cloud Native community, and even met some new ones.

Here's a recap of my trip!

## Edinburgh

My first stop was Edinburgh, for [KCD UK](https://ckranz.github.io/kcduk/). This was my first-ever KCD UK, and my first trip to Edinburgh. I gave two talks at KCD UK. 

My first talk was a keynote. I was asked to keynote after someone dropped out unexpectedly. I mention this not because I resent not being asked first (I don't!), but rather, because I'm so flattered that I was asked to fill in! An opportunity is an opportunity, and I am super grateful for this one!

My keynote was the third of three keynotes on Day 2, right after my good friend [Hazel Weakly](https://hazelweakly.me), who spoke about building and executing a successful engineering strategy at a large organization. Hazel is incredibly talented and is a tough act to follow!

My keynote was called, "From 'I Don't Know' to 'Let Me Show You How'". In it, I shared tips for a successful career in tech, drawing on my personal experiences from the past 25+ years of working in tech.

I first gave this keynote at KCD Porto in 2024. It was my first-ever keynote, and it was the opening keynote for the very first edition of KCD Porto. To say that I was nervous is an understatement. Not only that, it's not a technical talk per se. As woman in tech, I worried about it being too "fluffy" for a tech conference and falling into an "OF COURSE the girl gives a fluffy talk" stereotype. The things we tell ourselves...eeesh.

Anyway, the talk went over really well that year, and I even [turned the talk into a blog post](/post/from-i-don-t-know-to-let-me-show-you-how/), which went super viral. So I was pretty excited to have the opportunity to give this talk again.

Fast-forward to 2026 and AGAIN I was nervous giving this talk, and AGAIN, the talk went over super well. We really need tell those inner critics to shut the fuck up, yo.

Later that afternoon, I took the stage with [Kasper Borg Nissen](https://www.linkedin.com/in/kaspernissen/). It was our fourth time giving this talk together, and our first-ever collaboration. It's the gift that keeps on giving! The talk is called "Your Agent Did WHAT? Forensic Observability for Systems That Don't Leave Obvious Footprints." It explores the telemetry captured by AI agents and LLM API calls, so that we can better understand what's going on in our AI applications. It also talks about the [convergence of the mish-mash of AI semantic conventions towards OpenTelemetry GenAI semantic conventions](/post/otel-gen-ai-normalizer/).

We first débuted this talk in September of this year, for SREDay London. We gave it two more time in October in Prague, first for [Observability Summit EU](https://youtu.be/O9NQEuW09Bk?si=HfLMNZVvIXMjIyIz), and then for [Open Source Summit EU](https://sched.co/2Rafg). It was really cool to work on this talk, because it pushed me outside of my comfort zone, and forced me to learn all about observing LLMs and agents, and learned from Kasper as well. Kasper and I have cooked up a really cool follow-up talk which we've submitted to a few conferences for 2027, so I'm hoping we get a chance to deliver it! 🤞🤞

## Detour

After Edinburgh, I headed to Warsaw via Amsterdam. [Marcus Noble](https://www.linkedin.com/in/marcusnoble/) (whom I'd met earlier this year at Cloud Native Days Romania), and who was also at KCD UK, was also travelling from Edinburgh to Warsaw, for [Cloud Native Days (CND) Poland](https://cloudnativedayspoland.org/agenda/), and we were on the same flights. Yay for travel buddies!

Hazel was also on the same flight. We were also staying at the same hotel, so we rode to the airport together. Hazel was a good sport about arriving heinously early to the airport to humor my massive airport anxiety. You're a good friend, Hazel! 💜

On the same flight and sitting in the SAME ROW as me, was [William Rizzo](https://www.linkedin.com/in/william-rizzo/). I met William a few times in person at various KubeCons and KCDs. He is a sweetheart of a guy. We got to chatting on the flight, and William asked where I was off to. I told him that Marcus and I were heading to Warsaw and had a 5-hour layover in Amsterdamn.

So what does he do? He invites us to his house for lunch during our layover. I kid you not. He cooked us a delicious homecooked meal, we met his bunny, and he drove us back to the airport, in time for our connecting flight to Warsaw.

I am so touched by his act of kindness and hospitality. It is definitely the most memorable layover ever!

## Warsaw

Marcus and I made it to Warsaw without issue, and we even made it to the CND Poland speaker dinner that evening, just in time for us to dive into a big-ass platter of meat.

This was my second time in Warsaw, and my second time at CND Poland (known as KCD Warsaw last year). I fell in love with this conference last year because of the warmth of the Polish Cloud Native community, and because of the organizers. In particular, [Tomasz Cholewa](https://www.linkedin.com/in/cloudowski/) and [Pawel Piwosz](https://www.linkedin.com/in/pawelpiwosz/), whom I now consider friends. Which is why I was so damn tickled when Tomasz and Pawel asked [Josh Lee](https://www.joshuamlee.com) and me to be one of the keynotes at this year's conference.

We put together a brand-new keynote for this conference, "Zen AI Cohabitation: How I Learned to Embrace the Slop". This keynote is a roadmap for how to survive in this topsy-turvy AI world. Specifically, what to abdicate and delegate to AI without losing your ability to think for yourself. We also talk about how to navigate in the world of slop and what slop is okay versus not okay, and to make sure you still touch grass every so often. We had tons of fun putting this talk together, especially picking out the memes and coming up with fun capybara graphics. We we were also really happy with the response we got from the audience. 🤘

My original plan for Warsaw was to just attend CND Poland. And then I got an email from one of the organizers of [Observability Poland](https://observabilitypoland.pl/), who asked me to keynote at their event, taking place on the same day. I was already going to be in Waraw, and since the Observability Poland keynote would be in the morning and the CND Poland keynote was in the afternoon, I OF COURSE said yes. Was it a tad bit of madness? Yes it was. But no regrets.

The talk I gave was called, "OpenTelemetry: Playtime is Over". [I first gave this talk with Josh at KCD Czech & Slovak](https://www.youtube.com/live/xzD4cvBGTpQ?si=Urg4KGzuC7bZLOMV&t=1524). This time, I gave it solo (with Josh's blessing). This talk is an evolution of my blog post, ["We're Past the OpenTelemetry Honeymoon Period"](/post/we-re-past-the-opentelemetry-honeymoon-period/), in which I talk about some of the challenges that organizations face as they try to achieve the promise of Observability with OpenTelemetry, and some of the tools available to help with that. I feature tools like [OpAMP](/post/opentelemetry-opamp-for-you-and-me/), [OTel Weaver](/post/let-s-learn-about-otel-weaver-together/), and [OTel Arrow](/post/let-s-learn-about-otel-arrow-together/).

I have to give a huge shoutout to my friend and colleage, [Agata Włodarczyk](https://www.linkedin.com/in/agatawlodarczyk/), who took the train from Gdank to Warsaw for the day just to watch me speak at both conferences. I'm so grateful for the extra in-person hang time.

## Bergen

The final stop of my three-country tour was Bergen, Norway, for [CND Norway](https://2026.cloudnativedays.no/speaker/adriana-villela). I swapped one travel buddy for another. Marcus headed back home to the UK after Poland, and Josh and I travelled together to Norway. Given the amount of travel that we've done together, we'd never been on any flights together. Untl this trip.

Getting to Bergen was um...interesting. We were supposed to go to Bergen via Amsterdam, with a 2-hour connection. That is, until our flight from Warsaw to Amsterdam was delayed by 1.5 hours. The chances of making the connection were slim to none. So we ended up getting on a different set of flights that would still get us to Bergen that day (albeit waaaay later), this time via Copenhagen. Let's just say that having a travel buddy and lounge access was super helpful for this particular trip.

This was my second time in Bergen for CND Norway (known as Cloud Native Bergen last year). This is an incredible conference in an absolutely gorgeous city which is flanked by mountains. One of the highlights of this conference is the speaker dinner. It takes place at a restaurant called [Skyskraperen Restaurant](https://ulriken643.no/en/skyscraper), atop [Mount Ulriken](https://en.visitbergen.com/things-to-do/hike-up-mount-ulriken-p1710093). Speakers have the option of taking the gondola up, or, if you feel adventurous, you can join organizer [Hans Kristian Flaatten](https://www.linkedin.com/in/hansflaatten/) on a hike up the mountain. I did the hike last year, and did it again this year. As one does.

[![View post on LinkedIn](/images/posts/edi-waw-bgo-conference-recap/cnd-bergen.png)](https://lnkd.in/p/ejgQBW8i)


Last year [Diana Todea](https://www.linkedin.com/in/diana-todea-b2a79968/) was my climbing ~~victim~~ buddy. Unlike last year, this year, Diana opted to take the gondola. [Cortney Nickerson](https://www.linkedin.com/in/cortney-nickerson-26836413a/) was also part of our band of merry conference buddies and went the gondola route. So instead, Josh became this year's climbing ~~victim~~ buddy. What can I say? Travel with me involves two things: going up stairs/mountains/etc, and getting boba. It is the way.

The conference itself was tons of fun. I chatted with tons of people, made new friends, and got to know others a bit bitter.

Oh yeah, and the talk. This was my final talk of five. The talk is called "OpAMP For You and Me: The OpAMP Guide We All Desperately Needed", and was presented with Josh. It's an introduction to [OpAMP](/post/opentelemetry-opamp-for-you-and-me/), a protocol for managing and configuring data collection agents (e.g. OTel Collectors or vendor agents - not AI agents) at scale. OpAMP is one of those hidden gems of OTel that not a lot of people talk about, because they find it overwhelming and confusing. In this talk, Josh and I demystified OpAMP, and even gave a live demo.

Unfortunately, the live demo imploded partway through, because I blindly clicked on a pop-up to update VSCode mid-demo. Fortunately, Josh and I fielded questions while I got VSCode and my dev container back up and running. I'm surprised that I didn't panic during this ordeal. Past Adriana would've freaked the fuck out, and instsead, I was cool as a cucumber. Besides, I think that imploding demos are a right of passage. I'm now part of the club. 🙃

## Final Thoughts

I personally love the KCDs and CNDs. They're smaller community conferences that just feel so much more personal. While I normally get to see tons of friends when I attend a large conference like KubeCon, we're all so damn busy running around that we don't really have time to sit down for a proper chat. If you're lucky, you get a selfie with a friend before they rush off to the next thing they need to be at.

Which is why I enjoy community conferences so much. They're cozy. They're intimate. They're where some great magic happens. The 1:1 conversations lead to ideas for the next talk or collaboration. They lead to new friendships and reinforce the bonds of old ones.

And so, even though I was travelling solo for this trip, I was never really alone.
