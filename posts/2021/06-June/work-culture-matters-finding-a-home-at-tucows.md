### I still hadn’t found what I was looking for

I consider myself a non-corporate type. I like to dress casually most days, preferring graphic tees with witty things written on them. I despise sucking up to execs. I am highly allergic to BS. I need something, I want to be able to talk directly to the person who can make it happen — corporate hierarchy be damned. And yet, as allergic as I am to big corporate life, somehow, the bulk of my career has been spent in large corporations. I suppose that at some point I just figured that that’s the way things were. Over the years, it made me jaded and bitter.

For a long time, my tech career felt like it had plateaued. It seemed that everyone around me was progressing in their careers, and yet, there I was…stagnating. When would my career finally take off? Everyone else seemed to be getting director and VP positions. I was just a plain ‘ole software developer. What was wrong with me?

As it turns out, nothing! And it took an amazing friend and mentor to help me realize it.

### Changing Perspective

Bernard was the tech lead on my DevOps Coaching team from 2016 to 2019. He was a contractor on my team who worked at a large consultancy; however, he had worked with startups for most of his career, so his attitudes toward work were far from corporate. Bernard opened my eyes to the fact that yes, you could work in tech and be free from the shackles of corporate life. He taught me to rely less on email, and more on real-time chats (face-to-face, Slack, etc). He taught me that the “butts on seats” mentality was ridiculous, and that getting the work done on time was what mattered. He taught me to learn to manage my calendar to carve out time to do work and attend only meaningful meetings. He taught me everything I know about DevOps and SRE. He taught me to value my talents as a kick-ass software engineer and leader. Most importantly, he made me realize that I didn’t have to live by the stupid rules of corporate life. I finally had validation for my aversion to big corporate life!

In 2019, we went our separate ways job-wise. But we remained close friends. We had a dream. We both believed in the power of DevOps and SRE, and so, in our spare time, we worked on a platform for creating standardized and shareable SLO-driven automations for SREs. We started the [Dzero Labs blog](http://medium.com/dzerolabs) together. And while you may see my name on all of the blog posts, Bernard was always there behind the scenes. He was a sounding board for blog topics. He served as editor for my posts. He was my tech sparring partner (we had our fair share of passionate debates).

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/0*I-bBpHNjtJhpJQEz)

Bernard and me. Taken in 2019.

### Pivot

Bernard and I hoped that someday, we might be able to work on our platform full-time. Then, in mid-February of 2021, Bernard had a stroke. He’s been unresponsive ever since. He’s barely 40. It was a blow to my family and me. He was a good friend to all of us.

I was lost. I was devastated. Our dreams suddenly came to a grinding halt. Worst off, I’d lost my best friend, coding partner, and sounding board.

I slowly started to pull myself out of my funk, and decided that it was time to move on. I really didn’t like where I was working at the time. I can’t point to one specific thing. I guess I never really felt at home. The company I worked at prided itself on a good work culture, but to me, it was never more than okay. Though I learned a lot from a technical standpoint, it felt like just another corporate gig. More silos. More red tape. Meh work culture. The one thing it had going for it were the flexible work hours and remote work, even pre-pandemic. But I felt under-appreciated, under-utilized, and wholly uninspired.

Then a former co-worker and friend from my Java days referred me to Tucows. I have to admit that while I applied, I was at first skeptical. By that point, I’d talked to so many uninspiring companies that I was just jaded. It seemed like just another corporation. But there was something different about it.

My first inkling of that was in the application process. They cared to know how to pronounce my name, and what my preferred pronouns were. How cool was that?! My recruiter was super friendly. But not in a fakey fakey way that makes you cringe. While I had initially applied for a team lead position, the hiring team felt that I was better suited for a more strategic position as a technical manager. Color me flattered.

I actually enjoyed the interview process. The interviews felt like a series of fun, casual conversations. I clicked with my interviewers. I had some amazing chats with Hanno, Tucows’ CTO, and my now-manager, AJ. Our values seemed aligned. Most importantly, the folks at Tucows wanted to hire me for my unique, individual qualities. They saw what other potential employers failed to see — the value that I, as an individual, could bring to the table. One big selling point for me was that Tucows values individual contributors as much as they value managers, and therefore has a career path for individual contributors to grow. So many companies view management as the be-all end-all. Having spent the bulk of my career as a software engineer, and loving hands-on work, this was a big deal. It also tells me that should I ever want to switch gears to an individual contributor role, I still have a home here.

### I’m Home

I’ve been at Tucows for almost a month now, and so far, I’ve been enjoying myself. Sure, there’s a bunch of ramping up, as with any new job, but I’ve been able to hit the ground running. Every day feels like a fun adventure. Work doesn’t seem like work when you’re given space and autonomy. Even though onboarding was remote, I got a warm reception. The welcome stickie that came with my work laptop made me smile. Also, being able to choose a Mac as a work laptop was amazing.

What a lovely welcome stickie!

The welcome swag that I got was fun and whimsical. (I also got some other stuff besides the cows, but these are by far my favourite goodies.)

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/0*8kWjkeD2SwxLVndb)

I got other swag too, but this was my favourite! My daughter commandeered one of the cows for herself.

The day I started, I got super warm welcome messages on Slack from Hanno and AJ. First-day jitters? None. Warm fuzzy feelings? Yes!

I have to also point out that even the InfoSec training was cool. I know that I sound like a dork when I say this, but I genuinely liked it. This, from someone who groans at mandatory training. (Admit it, we all do.) The trainer, Alex, actually made security interesting, and took the time to explain WHY certain security policies were in place. We also had some security training videos in the form of fun cartoon videos. I genuinely wanted to watch them. (My family thought I was nuts.) Context matters, and this made me WANT to care.

You can really feel the fact that culture here is genuine, and it’s everything that I’ve been looking for my whole career. The focus is on productivity, and not on bureaucracy. While Tucows has almost 1000 employees, you’d never know it. The culture is non-stuffy, and has a startup-y fun vibe. Why? Because it comes from the top down. Both the CEO Elliott, and CTO Hanno have a chill vibe. I attended my first town hall in my second week, and I could feel it. Believe me, I am no fan of town halls. I typically find them boring and wasteful. This one wasn’t. I’m by no means a company groupie. I don’t believe in blind company love. A company has to earn my respect, through its actions above all else. Tucows has definitely earned my respect.

I contrast all of this with previous workplaces which tout themselves as having a “people-first” work culture, but don’t live up to the promise. Instead, they’ve served a dose of gender bias, information-hoarding, and power-grabbing. Yuck. 🤮

I was listening to an episode of the [o11ycast](https://www.heavybit.com/library/podcasts/o11ycast/) podcast last week (highly recommend!!), and something really stuck with me. The host talked about how there was no reason for anyone in tech to be stuck in a job that they hated, because there are so many wonderful companies out there. It’s just a matter of finding the right one. And she was right. I’ve been waiting for 20 years for my career to truly take off. That time has finally come. I’m excited to be working with a group of people who are willing to give me the autonomy and flexibility that I require, so that I can do my job to the best of my ability.

I am grateful to Bernard for making me feel comfortable in my own skin, for freeing me from the shackles of corporate life, and for helping me to embrace my talents. I miss our daily conversations dearly, but I will always have his spirit to guide me. The best way I can honour him and our friendship is to stay true to my values, to never be a corporate automaton, and to continue to push the boundaries of DevOps and SRE. I continue to hope for his recovery, and that someday, we’ll be able to pair program once again.

While I don’t know what the future holds, I can say that at this moment, I feel like I’ve found a good work home in Tucows.

My name is Adriana Villela, and I run the Observability and Platform Solutions teams at Tucows.

I’m super jazzed to be here, and I look forward to many tech adventures to come. For the first time in my career, I’m proud of where I work, and I’m excited to be a part of the Tucows Herd.
